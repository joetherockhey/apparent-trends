01PeakingTikTokInstagram

Ramalama Costume Walk

PeakPeaking now (mid to late Oct 2026), runs to 31 Oct

A low camera on the creator's feet as they stalk slowly towards the lens, then a hard cut on the beat reveals the full costume.

FormatLow-angle walk-up, one cut, full reveal

SoundRamalama (Bang Bang), Róisín Murphy

Why nowIt's the default costume-reveal sound for Halloween 2026. New Engen ranks it the #2 TikTok trend of October.

TimelineFirst seen 29 Jul 2025 and peaked around Halloween 2025 (one Oct 2025 video reached about 94M views). Revived from Aug 2026. Listed at the Peak stage with 456K videos in the third week of Oct 2026.

How a brand could use it

Swap the costume for a product, a mascot or a staff member in uniform. The slow walk-up builds suspense for any reveal, so it also works for a new menu item, a store fit-out or a packaging redesign.

A sneaker retailer films only the new shoe stalking down the aisle, then cuts to the full outfit on the drop.

Watch outHalloween is smaller in Australia, so lean on the 'big reveal' rather than the spooky angle. Check the track is cleared in the commercial music library before posting from a brand account.

02PeakingTikTok

Two Fishes

PeakPeaking in Oct 2026 (New Engen's audio of the month)

Creators lip-sync the Love Island 'two fish' speech to defend a questionable opinion with total conviction.

FormatLip-sync with an on-screen 'unpopular opinion'

SoundLove Island USA 'two fish' audio (credited to Sincere in most posts)

Why nowThe 'confidently wrong' tone gets people arguing in the comments, which pushes reach.

TimelineThe clip comes from Love Island USA Season 8 and was circulating by early Jul 2026. It took off again as a 'confidently wrong' lip-sync in Oct 2026 and spun off into Halloween costumes by 10 Oct.

How a brand could use it

Have the brand defend a harmless, slightly unhinged opinion about its own category, e.g. 'pineapple belongs on pizza'. It builds personality and comments without any product push.

A pizza chain's social lead lip-syncs it to defend pineapple, with the menu item in shot.

Watch outReality-TV audio, so check the clearance. Sources disagree on which islander is speaking, so don't name them in captions.

Text message from -A

PeakPeaked 23 Sep to early Oct 2026, still in mid-Oct round-ups

An on-screen 'text' signed '-A', Pretty Little Liars style, calls out a specific habit. Then the creator is caught doing exactly that.

FormatFake text overlay, then a caught-in-the-act clip

Soundpll purposes only ty (demi devito)

Why now2016 nostalgia is running hot and the cast joined in. The format slots into any niche.

TimelineWave started 19 Sep 2026. Shay Mitchell joined on 23 Sep and Lucy Hale opened her TikTok with it on 24 Sep. One post had 8.7M views by 29 Sep. Later, Ramdam and SocialBee were still listing it on 2, 5 and 14 Oct.

How a brand could use it

'-A' exposes the brand's fans: 'I know you added three more to cart at 1am. -A'. It also suits apps, as an exposé of a shortcut that users love.

A food-delivery client's '-A' texts call out the person ordering 'just a drink' and getting dessert as well.

Watch outIt's past peak, so post this week or skip it.

04PeakingTikTokInstagram

Getting robbed

PeakPeaking early to mid Oct 2026

A one-shot skit: someone is so absorbed in their phone that their bag gets stolen right behind them. The caption says what they were too distracted by.

FormatSingle locked-off shot with a text overlay

SoundOriginal sound, hector mort

Why nowOne take, no editing, any niche. Later flagged it as the easiest trend of mid-October.

TimelineThe original sound went up 26 Apr 2026 and brands like Vice Versa used it in Aug. Later listed it for Reels on 2 Oct and for TikTok on 16 Oct 2026.

How a brand could use it

Make the brand's content the distraction: 'me getting robbed because the new episode dropped'. It works for streaming, publishing, gaming, retail drops and sports.

A streaming client: a viewer glued to the season finale doesn't notice their bag, couch and dog leave the room.

Watch outKeep the theft obviously staged and comedic so it doesn't read as real crime footage.

I just want to be OK

PeakPeaking late Sep to mid Oct 2026

Creators dance or potter around happily while the caption reveals the stress they're ignoring.

FormatUpbeat clip plus a 'secretly stressed' caption

SoundBe OK, Ingrid Michaelson

Why nowRelatable 'quietly falling apart' humour is landing well as people head into end-of-year burnout.

TimelinePicked up through Aug and Sep 2026 on Ingrid Michaelson's 'Be OK'. Later listed it on 2 Oct, and New Engen and SocialBee had it in their mid-Oct round-ups (14 Oct).

Caption the stress your customer has and let the product be the reason they really are OK. It suits finance, insurance, telcos, travel and wellness.

Watch outKeep it light. The phrase is also used for sincere mental-health posts, so avoid anything that mocks real distress.

Netflix Documentary

PeakFirst peak Jul 2026 (TikTok), second wave on Instagram in Oct 2026

Sat in a chair under moody light with a documentary score, the creator narrates something mundane as if it were a true-crime confession.

FormatInterview-chair set-up, dramatic narration

SoundCinematic documentary score

Why nowInstagram is running its own second wave, and it's one of the most brand-friendly formats of the year.

TimelineStarted on TikTok on 16 Jun 2026. A 30 Jun video drew 11.5M views in two weeks, and brands like Dynamite Clothing jumped on in Jul. Anijam reported in Oct that it had crossed over and was trending on its own on Instagram.

How a brand could use it

Turn the brand's origin story or a tiny product detail into a solemn documentary. It's great for founders, craft brands and anyone with a quirky backstory.

A bakery owner, lit like a true-crime subject: 'It was 4am. The sourdough had not risen. We had 200 pre-orders.'

Watch outDon't use Netflix branding, logos or the 'N' sound. Keep the parody generic.

How this works

Each Monday, the latest round-ups from New Engen, Later, SocialPilot, SocialBee, Ramdam and others are cross-checked against each other and against live TikTok sound pages. Anything stale or hard to use for a brand is dropped. Peak dates come from the round-ups' own dates, sound-stage data and the upload dates of example posts.

Example images are the cover frames of the linked posts, shown for reference only. Where no example post could be captured, the image is an AI-generated illustration and labelled as one.

The descriptions and brand ideas are a starting point. Check a trend's sound and licensing before using it on a brand account.
